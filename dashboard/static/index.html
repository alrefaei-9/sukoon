Reading these numbers

Percent is how much smaller the sound pressure is at the earbuds than at the microphone: 6 dB is 50%, 20 dB is 90%, 40 dB is 99%.

Average attenuation compares all sound in and out over each 3 minutes, so it rises in a loud place and can go below zero in a quiet room where speech is boosted. A bang is measured over its first 80 ms. Sound leaking past the earbud seal is not included.

Each one is a sample where the mic hit its limit. 0 in the last 10 s is what you want. Steady counts while people just talk mean the level is too high.

Why mic level matters

Mic level is how strongly the sound card amplifies the microphone. Too high and close claps hit the limit (clipping), which sounds harsh and makes bangs harder to measure. Too low and quiet speech gets lost in noise.

The slider runs from 50% (−30 dB) to 100% (0 dB, the card’s maximum). Below 50%, normal speech a metre away drops toward the mic’s own hiss and the speech detector starts missing it. Turning it up can’t damage anything; it only makes clipping start sooner.

What the equalizer does

Each slider makes one range of pitches louder or quieter, from deep (bass) to crisp (clarity). 0 dB leaves it as it is.

This mic loses the high sounds that make speech crisp, and noise removal takes a little more. Raising presence and clarity brings them back.

Expert

Raw settings · the defaults suit normal use
How these work together

Sukoon removes background noise, then mixes a little of the plain microphone back in while someone talks, so you still hear the room naturally. The two room settings say how far that room sound sits below speech; Sukoon picks a value between them depending on how noisy it is.

When a sudden loud sound arrives, room sound is muted for the mute time. Test 5 also catches bangs while someone is talking: only the bang itself is turned down, usually for 30 to 100 ms, so speech before and after stays clear.

Sirens and other long loud sounds are played quieter, never removed. An alarm never plays below −26 dBFS, and a faint alarm is raised by up to 10 dB to reach that level (fixed values).
